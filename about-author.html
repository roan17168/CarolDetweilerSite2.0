Her Life & Story

Carol Detweiler has had a lengthy international and intercultural career, beginning with work as a Peace Corps Volunteer on a remote island in Micronesia for two years. Subsequently she worked with international students and as a volunteer with organizations involved in global education. She has traveled to more than fifty countries.

Having heard that refugees would be allowed to enter the U.S. from war-torn Kosova in 1999, and knowing some would be of college age, she immediately established a program to provide full college scholarships for more than 50 Kosovars, allowing them to continue their education. In recognition of this achievement, she was honored by the New York State Senate with a “Women of Distinction” award in 2000.

This book is based on the experiences of these refugees, as well as interviews with many others—including Kosova’s former president, Fatmir Sejdiu. Having made numerous trips to Kosova, along with extensive research into the region’s history and the war, she has sought through this historical fiction to bring alive the character of the Kosovar people and the love they have for their country.

She has lived across the U.S.—California, New Jersey, upstate New York, Washington DC, Michigan—and now lives in Oregon with her husband, Richard Detweiler, and their standard poodle. They have two daughters and a son, along with seven grandchildren.

The Iron Door & The Story Behind It

When the first Kosovar refugee students arrived in the United States in the summer of 1999, Carol witnessed young men and women step onto college campuses carrying only a single change of clothes in a small bag—and an immeasurable, invisible weight of trauma, lost family, and destroyed homelands.

"I didn't do the work for them. I simply unlocked a heavy iron door that they had to push through themselves." — Carol Detweiler

The metaphor of the "Iron Door" became the foundation of her mission: opening opportunities for education, while honoring the courage, determination, and resilience of the refugees who carried themselves forward to rebuild their futures.

The Journey

Early Career

Served as a Peace Corps Volunteer on a remote island in Micronesia for two years, beginning a lifelong dedication to intercultural understanding.

1999

Established a nationwide scholarship initiative across 25 U.S. colleges, awarding full four-year scholarships to over 50 Kosovar refugee students.

2000

Honored with the New York State Senate "Women of Distinction" Award for her humanitarian leadership on behalf of Kosovar students.

2004–2009

Conducted extensive research trips to Kosova, gathering firsthand testimonies and interviewing refugees and leaders, including President Fatmir Sejdiu.

2010–2020

Writing the historical fiction from the many facts, eyewitness accounts, and research she accumulated.

2025–2026

Published A Murder of Crows Fly with a Red Ribbon, sharing these preserved stories of survival, hope, and dignity with the world.
