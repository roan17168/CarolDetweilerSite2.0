The Scholarship Legacy

Unlocking the iron door for over 50 Kosovar refugee students in 1999.

One Act of Kindness Across Generations

In May 1999, as hundreds of thousands of ethnic Albanians were violently expelled from Kosova, Carol Detweiler recognized that among the refugees arriving in the United States were college-age youth whose education had been abruptly stripped away.

Working tirelessly with telephone and fax machines, Carol connected with social service agencies nationwide—including Catholic Charities, Lutheran Refugee Placement, and Jewish Family Services—and coordinated with 25 American colleges and universities willing to grant full four-year tuition, room, and board scholarships from their institutional budgets.

Recognition of Excellence

In 2000, the New York State Senate honored Carol Detweiler with the prestigious "Women of Distinction" Award for her visionary leadership in opening higher education to war refugees.

Where Are They Now?

Today, the students who pushed through that "heavy iron door" have become professional actors, educators, doctors, business leaders, and cultural ambassadors in both the United States and a free Kosova. Many have started families of their own, passing forward the gift of education and kindness.
