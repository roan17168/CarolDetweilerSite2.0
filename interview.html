In Her Own Words

Conversations with Carol Detweiler & Kushtrim Hoxha on the Libri Podcast

This Podcast was hosted by Anita and Berat Bllaca at their Libri bookstore/café in Yorktown Heights, New York.

Key Moments — Jump to Chapter

Click any timestamp below to jump directly to that conversation in the video above.

  • 04:02

    The One-Bag Reality

    Carol reflects on seeing refugee students arrive at college with a single change of clothes in one bag, while American students unloaded carloads.

  • 05:18

    The Invisible Baggage

    Exploring the profound emotional trauma of war, loss of family, and lost homeland that students carried silently beneath the surface.

  • 06:21

    Unlocking the Iron Door

    Carol describes her role simply as unlocking a heavy iron door that each student had to courageously push through to achieve their goals.

  • 10:38

    From Refugee Camp to Actor

    Kushtrim Hoxha recounts fleeing to Macedonia, arriving in California, and securing the scholarship that launched his acting career.

  • 14:59

    The Agony of Choice

    Carol shares the heartbreaking story of two sisters who turned down college scholarships to work in a chicken factory to support their parents.

  • 30:13

    The Symbolism of the Red Ribbon

    How the red ribbon threads together memory, faith, resilience, and the fragile hope that sustained refugees through displacement.

  • 39:45

    Crossing Into Freedom

    Carol reads an excerpt about arriving at JFK, crossing the Brooklyn Bridge, and carrying the internal weights of loss even after safety.

  • 49:50

    The Bitter Burning Pages

    A visceral reading depicting the burning of Albanian books and literature, leaving a bitter taste but an unbreakable hunger for learning.

  • 55:32

    The Spirit of Hospitality

    Carol reads from her 2004 journal reflection about visiting Kosova and discovering the legendary warmth and welcoming spirit of its people.

  • 59:11

    The Crows and the Ribbon

    Carol summarizes the central meaning of the book's title: the crows representing battlefield devastation and the ribbon representing hope.
